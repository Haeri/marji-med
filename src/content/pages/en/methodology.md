## Principle

MCP is cardio-synchronised electrical stimulation of skeletal muscle in counterpulsation mode. An ECG signal triggers the stimulation, and the resulting contraction compresses peripheral arteries and veins during diastole. The authors attribute the haemodynamic effect to this compression, with a decrease in systemic vascular resistance and an increase in cardiac output.

## Two pillars of the method

The inventor describes MCP as a hybrid of two established approaches: assisted circulation and electrical muscle stimulation (EMS). Assisted circulation is otherwise used mainly in the operating theatre and intensive care, and EMS can be unsuitable for some heart patients. According to the inventor, MCP can be used for most patients with cardiovascular disease and, with a portable device, for prevention at home.

## Setup

- A portable, battery-powered, microprocessor-controlled pulse generator
- A chest belt or ECG electrodes on the chest, depending on the device, for synchronisation with the heartbeat
- Four active motor electrodes on the treated muscles, which divide the area into four zones
- Two or four passive electrodes, depending on the device

Sensor electrodes may need an atypical position. What matters is a sharp signal from the R and T waves.

## Timing

Stimulation is delivered at a set delay after the R wave, known as the K-factor, so that the contraction falls in early diastole. If synchronisation fails and the contraction reaches the wrong phase of the cardiac cycle, afterload rises and the method can load the heart instead of unloading it.

In an animal study that tested different time points across systole and diastole using pressure-volume loops, the best results were seen at end-systole. The stimulation parameters published in that study were trains of 75 ms, made up of biphasic impulses 1 ms wide at 200 Hz, at 10 V and 30 V. These values come from anaesthetised pigs and are not a clinical protocol.

## Carousel stimulation

The four zones are activated one after another, one with each heartbeat. Every heartbeat is therefore supported (assisted circulation at 1:1), while each muscle group works for one heartbeat and rests for the next three (muscle stimulation at 1:4). According to the inventor, this lets even deconditioned muscle restore its energy and work without signs of fatigue.

## Stimulation intensity

Intensity is set according to the patient's condition, as a percentage of maximal tetanising force (MTF).

| Level | Response | MTF | Heart failure | Ischaemic heart disease | Shaping |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Above threshold | Very strong contraction | 100% | ~~Avoid~~ | ~~Avoid~~ | *Caution* |
| Above threshold | Strong contraction | 75% | ~~Avoid~~ | *Caution* | **Suitable** |
| Above threshold | Moderate contraction | 50% | *Caution* | **Suitable** | **Suitable** |
| Above threshold | Light contraction | 25% | **Suitable** | **Suitable** | **Suitable** |
| Threshold | Fibrillation | 0 | **Suitable** | **Suitable** | *Caution* |
| Below threshold | High sensation | −25% | **Suitable** | *Caution* | ~~Avoid~~ |
| Below threshold | Moderate sensation | −50% | *Caution* | *Caution* | ~~Avoid~~ |
| Below threshold | Slight sensation | −75% | *Caution* | ~~Avoid~~ | ~~Avoid~~ |
| Below threshold | First sensation | −100% | ~~Avoid~~ | ~~Avoid~~ | ~~Avoid~~ |

- **Chronic heart failure:** threshold level only, from −25% to +25% of MTF, under strict control of the rate-pressure product. According to the inventor, this off-loads the heart by up to 20% within 15 to 20 minutes.
- **Ischaemic heart disease:** low to medium levels above threshold.
- **Sports and aesthetic medicine:** high levels above threshold are used only in these fields.

## Published protocols

| Setting | Session | Course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After coronary bypass surgery | 30 minutes | Daily for the first eight postoperative days |
| Chronic heart failure | 30 minutes | Eight days |
| Chronic heart failure, acute effects | 45 minutes | Three consecutive days |
| Before anaesthesia | 15 minutes | Single session |

## Assessment

The clinical studies measured response with resting ECG, blood pressure, two-dimensional echocardiography and forearm impedance plethysmography, along with exercise duration and walking distance. The pre-anaesthesia study also used invasive haemodynamic monitoring.

## Warning signs during a session

According to the inventor, none of the following should appear during a session. Any of them points to an error in the method or in patient selection.

- Tachycardia or hypertension
- Dyspnoea or angina pectoris
- Discomfort or a feeling of heaviness in the chest

## Common misconceptions

The inventor identifies two beliefs as false, because they ignore the mechanisms of nervous and humoral regulation.

- That more muscle mass and stronger contractions produce a greater counterpulsation effect.
- That the procedure is pointless because the counterpulsation effect appears only during the session.
