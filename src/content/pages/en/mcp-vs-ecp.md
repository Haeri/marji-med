## The essential difference

External counterpulsation (ECP) compresses the legs from the outside, with pneumatic cuffs. Muscular counterpulsation (MCP) compresses the peripheral vessels from within, by making the leg muscles contract.

![Two cross-sections of a leg. In ECP an outer cuff presses inward on the limb. In MCP the muscle itself contracts around the central vessel.](/uploads/compression-diagram.svg)

*Cross-section of the limb, schematic. In ECP, a cuff (dark) presses inward from outside. In MCP, the stimulated muscle (red) contracts around the vessels. Stimulation comes through electrodes on the skin (dark bars).*

## Side by side

| | MCP | ECP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source of compression | The patient's calf and thigh muscles | Pneumatic cuffs on the legs |
| Muscles during treatment | Active | Passive |
| Preload | Undesirable increase can be avoided, according to the inventor | No comparable claim made |
| Applied through | Adhesive skin electrodes | Cuffs inflated from a compressor |
| Trigger | ECG | ECG |
| Timing | One of four zones stimulated with each heartbeat, in early diastole | Sequential inflation in diastole from the calves upward, deflation before systole |
| Equipment | Battery-powered, portable pulse generator | Stationary treatment unit |
| Course | 30 minutes daily for eight days in published studies | Typically 35 one-hour sessions |
| Vascular access | None | None |
| Evidence | Small studies, mainly postoperative and in heart failure | Longer clinical record, mainly in refractory angina |

## MCP, exercise and EMS

MCP is also often confused with physical training and electrical muscle stimulation (EMS). The inventor describes the difference as follows.

| | Muscles | Joints | Heart |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical exercise | Loaded | Loaded | Loaded |
| EMS | Gently loaded, selected groups | Not affected | Loaded |
| MCP | Gently loaded, selected groups | Not affected | Off-loaded |

## Reading the comparison

We have not identified a study comparing MCP and ECP directly, so this table describes the methods and should not be read as evidence that one is more effective than the other.

Each method needs to be judged for a specific indication. A Cochrane review, for example, found no convincing evidence to support routine use of ECP in acute ischaemic stroke. MCP has its own closest published comparison in an animal study with the intra-aortic balloon pump: in six calves, MCP and the balloon pump had the same effects on cardiac output and left ventricular stroke work, and MCP also reduced systemic vascular resistance.
