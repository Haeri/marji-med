## Studied settings

### After coronary bypass surgery

The largest published randomised study included 50 patients after coronary artery bypass grafting, who received standard care or standard care with MCP, for 30 minutes daily over the first eight postoperative days. [See the results](/results/#pmid-18793232).

### Chronic heart failure

A clinical study applied MCP for 30 minutes over eight days to patients under optimal therapy, with and without chronic heart failure. The largest effects were seen in patients with an ejection fraction below 40%. [See the results](/results/#pmid-18373318). An independent study in 22 patients looked at acute effects and safety over three days. [See the results](/results/#pmid-22145938).

### Before anaesthesia

A prospective pilot study applied a single 15-minute session before general anaesthesia in 17 coronary patients with impaired ejection fraction undergoing elective bypass surgery. [See the results](/results/#pmid-28804232).

### Rehabilitation in osteoarthrosis

MCP of the lower limbs has been used in lower limb osteoarthrosis, alone and combined with radon and iodine-bromine baths, with reported analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects and improved locomotor function. [See the results](/results/#pmid-17144553).

## Clinical indications

The lists below are the indications proposed by the inventor of the method, based on his clinical experience. Published studies cover only some of them.

### Cardiology

- Heart failure, acute or chronic, including congestive heart failure and oedema
- Multifocal atherosclerosis
- Coronary artery disease, chronic or acute (myocardial infarction and/or angina pectoris)
- Dilated cardiomyopathy
- Tachycardia of intra- or extra-cardiac origin
- Atrial fibrillation and other rhythm disorders
- Mitral or tricuspid valve incompetence
- Hypertension
- Prevention of ischaemic heart disease and chronic heart failure

### Angiology

- Peripheral arterial disease, atherosclerotic or diabetic
- Arterial insufficiency
- Lymphovenous insufficiency
- Elephantiasis
- Venous stasis (trophic) ulcers

### Surgery

- Preoperative preparation, long or short term
- Intraoperative support during anaesthesia and bypass
- Postoperative recovery in intensive care, hospital and outpatient care
- Local inflammatory complications
- Pressure ulcers

### Other

- Sanatorium and resort therapy, and rehabilitation
- Chronic fatigue syndrome

## Patient selection

MCP can be considered for cardiovascular patients without contraindications. The inventor suggests these groups in particular, and regards combined treatment with drugs, intervention or surgery as best for the patient.

### Alongside surgery or intervention

- Patients not eligible for angioplasty or bypass surgery
- Patients with recurrent angina after surgery
- Patients with haemodynamic complications after intervention or surgery
- Elderly and high-risk patients
- Patients with combined pathologies
- Patients who refuse operative treatment

### Alongside drug treatment

- Patients without improvement under maximal drug treatment
- Patients with allergies or side effects from medicines
- Patients unwilling to have conservative treatment

## Contraindications

### Absolute

- Bleeding
- Aortic and arterial aneurysms
- Malignant tumours
- Epilepsy and convulsions
- Stroke, and the first three months after stroke
- Spastic paralysis and paresis

### Relative

- Left ventricular aneurysm with risk of embolisation
- Hypertrophic and restrictive cardiomyopathy
- Left ventricular diastolic dysfunction
- Aortic insufficiency, grade III to IV
- Aortic stenosis
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Deep vein thrombosis
- Significant arrhythmia, and heart rate below 43 or above 170 beats per minute
- Non-malignant tumours, such as uterine fibromyoma
- Pregnancy and menstruation
- Acute infections and inflammation
- Cognitive dysfunction requiring medical supervision

### Implanted cardiac devices

In one study of 22 patients with chronic heart failure, no inappropriate sensing or pacing artifacts were detected in patients with implanted rhythm devices. [See the results](/results/#pmid-22145938). *The clinical recommendation for patients with pacemakers and defibrillators is to be confirmed by the clinical author.*
